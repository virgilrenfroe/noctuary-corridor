Motorized Cue Tip Shaper

DIY concept — fixed-radius abrasive cup + axial spin. Separates shaping from scuffing. Keeps nickel/dime dome geometry without wrist bias.

Problem

Hand scuffing and shaping with sandpaper or a tip tool depends on wrist angle and how you rotate the cue. Result: an oval or flat-spotted dome. Tip contact point then changes with cue rotation → inconsistent English and throw feel. You want a repeatable sphere segment (nickel ≈ 10.5 mm R, dime ≈ 9 mm R), then a light scuff for chalk grip — not the same motion doing both jobs.

Design principle

  • Fixed-radius abrasive cup — cavity is a spherical segment; tip is forced to that radius.
  • Axial spin — gearmotor turns the cup (or the cue) about the cue axis so abrasion is rotationally symmetric.
  • Constant pressure — light spring + depth stop; no leaning on the tool.
  • Timed cycle — 5–15 s shape pass; stop when stop bottoms out or timer ends.

Geometry does the rounding. You only clamp, press, and time.

Build steps

  1. CAD the cup cavities — spherical caps: nickel R≈10.5 mm, dime R≈9 mm; cup ID slightly larger than tip OD; leave wall for grit adhesive.
  2. Print/machine frame — rigid base, motor mount coaxial with collet axis, linear slide or flexure for axial approach.
  3. Mount gearmotor — shaft to cup adapter (set screw / flats); confirm runout <0.2 mm at cup face.
  4. Fit collet — soft contact on ferrule only; tip protrudes free into cup; never clamp the tip leather.
  5. Add spring + depth stop — spring between slide and stop; dial stop so shaping removes chalk glaze / high spots, not half the tip.
  6. Line cups — PSA abrasive in cavity; burnish so grit follows sphere. Flat pad for scuff head.
  7. Wire power + timer — low-torque start; optional series resistor or PWM soft-start.
  8. Dry-run on scrap tip — verify concentricity, pressure, and that ferrule never contacts grit.

How evenness is guaranteed

  • Cup is a rigid sphere segment — only that radius can touch when tip seats.
  • Spin about cue axis averages any grit high spots around the circumference.
  • Spring + stop fix pressure and depth; no hand rocking.
  • Oval tips get cut down to the cup radius; already-round tips barely abrade.

Human wrist is removed from the geometry loop.

Scuff = separate light pass

  • Swap to flat pad (or flip a dual-face head).
  • 1–3 s, lighter spring or stop backed off — raise nap for chalk, don’t reshape.
  • Never scuff with the concave cup if you want a true dome; cup shapes, pad textures.

Shaping fixes geometry; scuffing only adds micro-tooth.

Safety

  • Eye protection — leather and grit dust.
  • Low torque / low RPM — enough to abrade tip, not enough to wrench a jammed cue out of your hand; fuse or current-limit the motor.
  • Don’t chew the ferrule — depth stop and soft jaws; grit must stop short of fiber/phenolic. If you hear hard plastic, stop immediately.
  • Unplug when swapping cups or adjusting collet. Keep fingers clear of spinning cup.

Out of scope

Workshop / personal prototype only. Not a product design package — no certifications, no retail packaging, no claims about tournament legality of tip condition. Validate on sacrificial tips before touching a playing cue. Iterate CAD after first print (runout, spring rate, grit adhesion).
